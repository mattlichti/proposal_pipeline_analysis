Resolution initiating an amendment to the landmark designation of 429-431 Castro
Street (aka Castro Theatre), Assessor's Parcel Block No. 3582, Lot No. 085, under
Article 10 of the Planning Code.
WHEREAS, Under Planning Code Section 1004.1, the Board of Supervisors (Board)
may by Resolution initiate a Landmark designation; and
WHEREAS, The Castro Theatre was designated as a Landmark, #100, under Article
i 0 of the Planning Code, pursuant to Ordinance No. 355-77, passed by the Board on juiy 25,
WHEREAS, The Castro Theatre, built in the Spanish Colonial architectural style in
1922, is an exceptionally fine example of 1920's theatre design and represents a major work
by one of San Francisco's more prominent architects, Timothy Pfleuger; and
WHEREAS, The symmetrical facade is divided vertically into three roughly equal
portions, with the major element of the design being a large mullioned window, nearly as high
as the building itself, that is flanked by two smaller ones at mezzanine level; and
WHEREAS, The facade is painted a pale buff and pale pink colors, believed to
represent the original color scheme; and
WHEREAS, The exterior features a free-standing ticket office within a recessed
entryway with original tilework, the neon blade sign with animated Castro lettering and
intricate marquee over the sidewalk, both dating from the 1930's; and
WHEREAS, Publicly accessible interior spaces include a shallow front foyer with
stairways on both sides, a mezzanine lounge with a vaulted ceiling and a spacious auditorium
with a balcony; and
WHEREAS, Notable original features within the auditorium include a highly decorated
lintel and pair of columns framing its proscenium opening, organ screens adjacent to the
stage with small balconies, sidewalls with large sgraffito murals featuring a pair of colonnades
with fountains and a ceiling designed to resemble a tent with cast plaster features including
swags, ropes, tassels, wood flooring, interior and exit doors, and other details; and
WHEREAS, The Castro Theatre's ornate interior exhibits a high level of craftsmanship
and continues to convey the feeling of a 1920's era theater; and
WHEREAS, Incorporating live organ music into regular film programming is a long
tradition unique to the Castro Theatre, with a raisable organ console platform at the front of
the stage facing the screen; and
WHEREAS, Since inception the Castro Theatre has been under continuous ownership
by San Francisco's oldest movie-business family, the Nassers; and
WHEREAS, The Castro Theatre is located in the heart of the Castro District, an
important center of San Francisco's LGBTQ+ movement in the 1970s, which became a
national model for the struggle of the LGBTQ+ community for acceptance in the late 20th
century; and
WHEREAS, The Castro Theatre is one of San Francisco's longest operating and most
iconic queer social spaces, hosting a myriad of LGBTQ-themed events, including movies, film
festivals, concerts, sing-a-longs, drag shows, and other events; and
WHEREAS, For decades the Castro has featured films catering to a queer audience,
including showcasing works of gay directors including Andy Warhol, John Waters, Jean
Cocteau and Pier Paolo Pasolini, and hosting the premiere of the first feature-length
documentary about gay and lesbian identity, "Word Is Out: Stories of Some of Our Lives" in
WHEREAS, Since 1981 the Castro Theatre has served as the primary venue for the
San Francisco International LGBTQ+ Film Festival (also known as the Frameline Film
Festival), which is the longest running and largest LGBTQ+ film exhibition event in the world;
and
WHEREAS, Two Oscar winning films on late gay Supervisor Harvey Milk premiered at
the Castro Theatre, including "The Times of Harvey Milk" in 1984 and "Milk" in 2008; and
WHEREAS, The year 2022 marks the centennial of the Castro Theatre, which remains
among San Francisco's most iconic landmarks and an anchor cultural institution in the Castro
neighborhood and LGBTQ+ community; now, therefore, be it
RESOLVED, That the Board of Supervisors hereby initiates an amendment to the
landmark designation of the Castro Theatre, located at 429-431 Castro Street (Assessor's
Block/Lot 3582/085); and, be it
FURTHER RESOLVED, That the Board of Supervisors requests that the Planning
Department prepare a Landmark Designation Report to submit to the Historic Preservation
Commission for its consideration of the full historical, architectural, aesthetic and cultural
interest and value, including but not limited to the interior, of the Castro Theatre; and, be it
FURTHER RESOLVED, That the Board of Supervisors requests that the Historic
Preservation Commission consider what amendments to the landmark designation are
warranted to capture the property's full historical significance, and submit its recommendation
to the Board of Supervisors, according to Article 10 of the Planning Code.
