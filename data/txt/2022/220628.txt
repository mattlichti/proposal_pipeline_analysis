Resolution supporting California State Assembly Bill No. 288 (AB 288), introduced by
Assembly Members Lisa Calderon (D-25) and Mia Bonta (D-18) on ; AB
288 would prohibit the displacement of financial aid awards due to receipt of private
scholarships of students who are eligible for the: 1) Federal Pell Grant and/or 2)
California Dream Act financial aid at both public and private of higher education in the
State of California.
1O WHEREAS, Postsecondary education is a critical factor in promoting upward
economic mobility, especially for poor and low-income individuals; and
WHEREAS, While college enrollment has increased exponentially in the last several
decades, the enrollment and graduation rates of poor and low-income students remain
significantly behind those of their middle- and upper-income peers; and
WHEREAS, Experts resoundingly agree that California has a college affordability
problem, despite the state having long prioritized keeping tuition low or even free; and
WHEREAS, Affordability challenges contribute to inequities in college enrollment,
completion, and student debt burdens, with low-income and minority students less likely to
enroll in or complete college, and more likely to have borrowed for college; and
WHEREAS, Of California's students pursuing bachelor's degrees and with family
incomes of less than $50,000, 95% have unmet need even when packaged with self-help,
without self-help, 97% have unmet need, thereby requiring students to come up with
additional money that may be covered by a private scholarship; and
WHEREAS, Private scholarships assist students in covering non-tuition costs, which
are significant and often uncovered by the Cal Grant program; and
WHEREAS, The City and County of San Francisco created the pioneering
Kindergarten to College (K2C) child savings account program to orient students and families
toward college and address inequities in postsecondary access and success - with the first
K2C class poised to graduate from high school in 2023; and
WHEREAS, Mayor London Breed's Bridge to Excellence Program provides
scholarships to highly-motivated graduating high school seniors from low-income and under-
resourced communities to help overcome financial barriers they face attending college; and
WHEREAS, Scholarship displacement is a practice where colleges and universities
displace previously awarded financial aid when a student is awarded a private scholarship,
effectively reducing the amount of financial aid awarded and resulting in a zero net gain for
the student - a practice that many students are not aware of and don't realize will impact them
when they receive an external private scholarship; and
WHEREAS, AB 288, known as the California Ban on Scholarship Displacement Act,
aims to ensure that private scholarships supplement, and do not supplant, gift aid, grants,
scholarships, tuition waivers, and fellowship stipends provided by institutions of higher
education to students who have financial need and are eligible for the federal Pell Grant
and/or California Dream Act financial aid; and
WHEREAS, Prohibiting the practice of scholarship displacement is essential to make
college more affordable, especially in light of the COVID-19 pandemic, as public and private
resources become even more limited for low-income students; now, therefore, be it
RESOLVED, That the City and County of San Francisco supports Assembly Bill No.
288 that would prohibit the displacement of financial aid awards due to receipt of private
scholarships of students who are eligible for the Federal Pell Grant and/or California Dream
Act financial aid at both public and private of higher education in the state of California; and,
be it
FURTHER RESOLVED, That the San Francisco Board of Supervisors supports
Assembly Bill No. 288, the California Ban on Scholarship Displacement Act; and, be it
FURTHER RESOLVED, That the Clerk of the Board of Supervisors of the City and
County of San Francisco transmit a copy of this Resolution to San Francisco's State
Legislative Delegation, City and County of San Francisco State Lobbyist, California Governor
Gavin Newsom and the Bill's primary sponsors Assembly Members Lisa Calderon and Mia
Banta.
