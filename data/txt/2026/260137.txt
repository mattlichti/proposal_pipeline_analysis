SUBSTITUTED
 

Resolution imposing interim zoning controls for 18 months to require a Conditional
Use authorization for the establishment of new Convenience Store uses in the
Tenderloin and South of Market Public Safety Zone, as both terms are herein defined;
affirming the Planning Department's determination under the California Environmental
Quality Act; and making findings of consistency with the eight priority policies of
Planning Code, Section 101.1.
WHEREAS, Planning Code, Section 306.7, authorizes the Board of Supervisors to
impose interim zoning controls to temporarily suspend the processing of certain land use
authorization applications that may conflict with a contemplated zoning proposal which the
Board is considering, studying, or intends to study within a reasonable time; and
WHEREAS, Interim zoning controls are necessary to ensure that the legislative
scheme which may ultimately be adopted is not undermined during the planning and
legislative process by the approval or issuance of permits authorizing uses that may conflict
with that scheme; and
WHEREAS, As set forth in recent legislation in Board , there is an
epidemic of opioid addiction and use in San Francisco, as in other major U.S. cities; this
epidemic is particularly serious in the Tenderloin and the South of Market neighborhoods,
which have some of the City's highest rates of drug-related crime, including narcotics offenses
and gun-related crimes frequently connected with drug offenses; and
WHEREAS, The Tenderloin is also home to a high density of low- and middle-income
families, including many families with children; and
WHEREAS, The South of Market neighborhood, which contains a mix of residential,
commercial, hospitality, and entertainment uses that generate late-night pedestrian and
vehicle activity, features a Sixth Street corridor that is among the highest-profile vehicular
entry points to downtown San Francisco from Interstate 280; and
WHEREAS, The high rate of drug-related crime in the Tenderloin and South of Market
leads individuals and families that live, work, and attend school in the area to face health and
safety risks, including exposure to drugs, needles, human waste, uncontained refuse, and
other hazardous materials that are left on sidewalks and streets; and
WHEREAS, People who live, work, and attend school in the area have expressed fear
about exposure to drug sales and use, gunshots, physical assault, and intimidation by
individuals or groups engaged in illegal activity, and property crimes such as theft and
damage to storefronts and vehicles; and
WHEREAS, Outdoor, public drug sales and use in the Tenderloin and South of Market
are common and cause particularly acute public health and safety risks at night and in the
early hours of the morning, when large groups gather in lighted areas adjacent to open retail
food and tobacco businesses to engage in drug sales and use; and
WHEREAS, Large gatherings of people engaged in drug sales and use are particularly
prevalent in the area of the Tenderloin and South of Market that is bounded by the following
street segments, inclusive of properties abutting both sides of the streets: South Van Ness
Avenue between Market Street and Thirteenth Street, Thirteenth Street between South Van
Ness Avenue and Folsom Street, Folsom Street between Thirteenth Street and Third Street,
Third Street between Folsom Street and Market Street, Market Street between Third Street
and Powell Street, Powell Street between Market Street and Geary Street, Geary Street
between Powell Street and Polk Street, Polk Street between Geary Street and McAllister
Street, McAllister Street between Polk Street and Larkin Street, Larkin Street between
McAllister Street and Market Street, and Market Street between Ninth Street and Van Ness
Avenue (an area defined herein as the "Tenderloin and South of Market Public Safety Area").
A diagram of the approximate boundaries of the Tenderloin and South of Market Public Safety
Area is provided in the Police Department report on file with the Clerk of the Board of
Supervisors in File Nos. 251251 and 260137, and is fully incorporated herein by reference;
and
WHEREAS, The Police Department has analyzed 2025 incident reports for narcotics
crimes and other crimes frequently connected with drug activity and determined that a
relatively high concentration of such crimes was reported in the Tenderloin and South of
Market Public Safety Area; and
WHEREAS, The Police Department has also observed that, compared with similar
sized areas in the City, including other areas in the Tenderloin, the Tenderloin and South of
Market Public Safety Area has experienced the greatest concentration of large groups
engaged in drug sales and use in public, outdoor spaces; and
WHEREAS, Large groups of people engaged in drug sales and use in the Tenderloin
and South of Market Public Safety Area generally form and congregate in the vicinity of retail
businesses selling food and tobacco products, especially at night; and
WHEREAS, These retail food and tobacco establishments in effect facilitate the
nighttime drug market by providing a lighted gathering point for drug users and dealers, by
enabling drug users and dealers to take cover indoors to avoid police patrols, and, in some
cases, by selling tobacco, tobacco paraphernalia, and other retail goods used by people
engaged in drug use and sales; and
WHEREAS, The gatherings of large groups engaged in drug activity in the Tenderloin
and South of Market Public Safety Area present particular health and safety risks because
these large groups contribute to property crimes and violent crime in the area, leave large
amounts of debris on the streets and sidewalks and create unsanitary conditions, leading to
feelings of anxiety and fear among residents of the area, especially seniors and families with
children; and
WHEREAS, Large groups engaged in drug use and sales at night also present serious
challenges to effective law enforcement, as these large groups are difficult for the police to
control and pose safety risks for officers where the people engaged in illegal activity typically
outnumber police officers on patrol; and
WHEREAS, In August 2024, following adoption of the ordinances in Board File
Nos. 240407 and 240696, the City implemented a two-year pilot program restricting the
operating hours of retail food and tobacco establishments in a defined area of the Tenderloin,
referred to as the "Tenderloin Public Safety Area," to address late-night conditions associated
with drug activity and related crime in a section of the Tenderloin; and
WHEREAS, Since implementing the pilot program, the Police Department has reported
improvements in public safety and neighborhood conditions within the Tenderloin Public
Safety Area, including a 14% reduction in violent crime and narcotics incidents within the
Tenderloin Public Safety Area specifically; and
WHEREAS, Based on those improvements, the City decided to broaden the pilot
program to the Tenderloin and South of Market Public Safety Area, in response to the similar
problems occurring there, and in early 2026, extended both the time and the geographical
scope of the pilot, in Board ; and
WHEREAS, As part of that legislation, the Board of Supervisors urged the Office of the
Mayor, the Police Department, and other City departments, as applicable, to continue to
undertake enforcement efforts against businesses that are engaged in conduct that is already
illegal, including illegal onsite gambling, fraud, and health and safety violations; and
WHEREAS, These interim controls aim to do just that - to provide time for the City to
consider appropriate land use controls to help alleviate these problems; and
WHEREAS, There is a large number of retail establishments selling food items and
tobacco products in the South of Market and Tenderloin neighborhoods - more than 75
according to recent counts, eight of which are on the Sixth Street corridor alone; and
WHEREAS, Outside of these retail establishments large groups of people tend to
congregate, especially at night, contributing to numerous peace, health, safety, and general
welfare problems in the area, including excessive and dangerous loitering, littering, drug
trafficking, illegal gambling, public intoxication, vandalism, pedestrian obstructions, and traffic,
parking, and noise impacts on public streets, as detailed above; and
WHEREAS, These problems lead to the deterioration of the neighborhoods, the
devaluation of property, and the erosion of community values and quality of life; and
WHEREAS, The number and concentration of these establishments discourage more
desirable and needed commercial uses and limit opportunities for community-serving retail
and access to healthy food; and
WHEREAS, These conditions constitute an immediate threat to the public health,
safety, and welfare, warranting temporary zoning controls prohibiting new such uses while the
City studies permanent regulations; and
WHEREAS, The interim controls established by this Resolution will allow time for the
orderly completion of a planning study and for the adoption of appropriate legislation that
addresses these issues; and
WHEREAS, The Board has considered the impact on the public health, safety, and
peace, and general welfare if these interim controls are not imposed; and
WHEREAS, The Board has determined that the public interest will best be served by
the imposition of these interim controls to ensure that the legislative scheme which may be
ultimately adopted is not undermined during the planning and legislative process for
permanent controls; and
WHEREAS, The Board finds that these interim controls are consistent with the Priority
Policies set forth in Planning Code, Section 101.1, particularly Policies 1 and 2, because
these interim controls will preserve and enhance existing neighborhood character and protect
the cultural and economic vitality of these neighborhoods, and preserve and enhance
neighborhood-serving retail uses; and
WHEREAS, The Planning Department has determined that the actions contemplated in
this Resolution comply with the California Environmental Quality Act (California Public
Resources Code, Sections 21000 et seq.); said determination is on file with the Clerk of the
Board of Supervisors in and is incorporated herein by reference; the Board
affirms this determination; now, therefore, be it
RESOLVED, That for purposes of these interim controls, the following definitions shall
apply:
"Convenience Store" shall mean a use that contains less than 5,000 square feet and is
located in the Tenderloin and South of Market Public Safety Area that is (a) a Food Product
and Marketing Establishment as defined in Health Code, Section 440(a) that devotes more
than 50% of its gross floor area for the sale of packaged goods; (b) an establishment that sells
any Tobacco Product as defined in Health Code, Section 19H.2; and/or (c) a Tobacco
Paraphernalia Establishment as that term is used in Sections 249.5, 719, 723, and 7 44 of the
Planning Code. Convenience Stores shall not include Specialty Grocery uses as defined in
Planning Code, Section 102.
"Tenderloin and South of Market Public Safety Area" shall have the same meaning as
that term has in Board of Supervisors , that is, the area of the City bounded by
the following street segments, inclusive of properties abutting both sides of the streets: South
Van Ness Avenue between Market Street and Thirteenth Street, Thirteenth Street between
South Van Ness Avenue and Folsom Street, Folsom Street between Thirteenth Street and
Third Street, Third Street between Folsom Street and Market Street, Market Street between
Third Street and Powell Street, Powell Street between Market Street and Geary Street, Geary
Street between Powell Street and Polk Street, Polk Street between Geary Street and
McAllister Street, McAllister Street between Polk Street and Larkin Street, Larkin Street
between McAllister Street and Market Street, and Market Street between Ninth Street and
Van Ness Avenue. A diagram depicting the approximate boundaries of the Tenderloin and
South of Market Public Safety Area is provided in the Police Department report on file with the
Clerk of the Board of Supervisors in File Nos. 251251, and No. 260137 and is incorporated
herein by reference; and, be it
FURTHER RESOLVED, That, in the Tenderloin and South of Market Public Safety
Area, a proposed Convenience Store shall require Conditional Use authorization pursuant to
Planning Code, Section 303, while these Interim Zoning Controls are in effect; and, be it
FURTHER RESOLVED, That in addition to the findings required by Planning Code,
Section 303, in granting the Conditional Use authorization, the Planning Commission shall
also consider:
(a) Whether the proposed use serves a demonstrated neighborhood need by
providing goods or services that support the daily needs of residents, such as access to fresh
food, healthy groceries, or other essential items, and whether it contributes to a balanced mix
of neighborhood-serving commercial uses;
(b) Whether the number, proximity, and concentration of similar uses in the
immediate area would be compatible with the surrounding neighborhood and would not
discourage the viability of more desirable, community-serving businesses;
(c) Whether the physical design of the establishment promotes visibility and positive
street activation, including maintaining clear, transparent windows, avoiding dark or obscured
storefronts, and providing adequate lighting that supports safety without attracting nuisance
activity;
(d) Whether the proposed hours of operation are clearly defined and appropriately
tailored to the surrounding context, including consideration of whether late-night or early
morning operations could contribute to adverse conditions in the area;
(e) Whether the proposed use aligns with the City's public safety and public health
goals for the Tenderloin and South of Market Public Safety Area, including efforts to reduce
open-air drug markets and improve neighborhood conditions.
FURTHER RESOLVED, That these interim zoning controls shall remain in effect for a
period of 18 months from the date of imposition, unless they are extended or otherwise
amended in accordance with the provisions of Planning Code, Section 306. 7, or until the
adoption of permanent legislation regulating Convenience Stores in the Tenderloin and South
of Market Public Safety Area, whichever first occurs; and, be it
FURTHER RESOLVED, That while the planning study is underway, the Board urges
the Planning Department to evaluate what controls are best suited to ensure that
Convenience Stores in the Tenderloin and South of Market Public Safety Area serve
neighborhood needs and do not contribute to the crime, vandalism, and other public health
and safety issues identified in these interim controls; and, be it
FURTHER RESOLVED, That these interim controls shall be subject to extension,
modification, or replacement by further action of the Board of Supervisors.
